## Why optimize?
There is an intense, burning desire, in a select subset of people, to constantly optimize certain things in life. Whether it be opportunities, investment portfolios, time and energy spent, or simply doing less work.

There is always a reason for someone to try to optimize something to their benefit. (For the CS kids, ignore algorithms and leetcode for a little while, that'll come later)

But what does it mean to optimize something? What is the best "method" to optimize something? What would we be optimizing for?

## What does it mean to optimize something?
To optimize something is to do something to improve a desired metric or achieve a certain goal faster.

**Local Optimality**: To optimize towards the greatest possible result in the short term. Can be as short as this second, to the entire span of your life.

**Global Optimality**: To optimize towards the greatest possible result in the long term. Possibly all the way into infinity, or whatever frame of reference our minds can comprehend.

It seems more reasonable to conclude that, semantics aside, in the context of one's life, it can be more easily defined as:

**Local Optimality**: Success within institutional stints (Any tier of education, Jobs, Businesses, etc), ranging to days, months, quarters, years and decades.

**Global Optimality**: The success, impact, wealth or happiness of one's own life, or perhaps also the well being of their loved ones, across one's entire life.

## Does doing what is best for you right now, lead to the best outcome?
1. Maybe studying to get the highest GPA possible, will give you more opportunities in the future compared to anything else?
2. Maybe working longer at your job, or your startup, or studying harder to get the best certifications, will give you a higher chance of a promotion, a raise, more income?
3. Maybe saving up as much as possible, to be able to retire early and comfortably, is the best way to prepare for yourself and your aging family?

I hope you've noticed a key word in every one of those very agreeable statements.

"Maybe"

We can lie to each other as much as we want, but there is no certainty to life, we may tell ourselves in the moment, that yes, these are definitely things to aspire for, because there is a clear and measurable outcome that we want.

Maybe we need the extra motivation, maybe we all have to lie to ourselves a little to get by. But I hope you can admit as well as I have, that, nothing is guaranteed in life, we can position ourselves in the best possible manner and method, and nothing will ever be certain in life.

I'm not saying that these aren't things we shouldn't aspire towards, but I would like you to have this takeaway:

## Nobody can predict the future.
What is locally optimal to you, might be to spend all your time studying for a master's degree, maybe joining some networking event, perhaps even freelancing.
If you are optimizing towards career success, or income, that makes perfect sense.

But...

Will having these things make you happy later on? (See [Affective Forecasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affective_forecasting#Overview)) 

Are you trying to achieve these things to be happy? Having prosperity and stability is great, but is that all you need to be happy? Do you have any sense of purpose to be filled? How "successful" do you have to be before you can stop and finally enjoy what is left of your life?

If you are optimizing for happiness, are you sure the future you are building for yourself is a happy one?

To optimize for wealth in the short term intending to reach for "happiness" later on is a trap. Because there will never be enough wealth, and you will never stop chasing it. 

Money buys freedom, but don't sell all your freedom for money.

Something that you might aim for, or do, might be able to achieve something that you can see in your "local" neighourhood of perspective, you can imagine this to be a locally optimal route that you can take. Even so, it's not guaranteed that you can achieve this local goal that you can identify, even with all the effort you put in, you can only maximize your chances.

Maybe in the span of your life, there are even greater things that you could achieve, if you had simply embarked on another path, or tried something else.

Maybe a detour in something you found interesting could change the course of your life in ways you could have never imagined? Maybe learning something completely unrelated now could somehow help in the future?

"Maybe" is a word that comes up again. I don't know. You don't know. No one could possibly know.

No one can know unless they try.

## What is the best "method" to optimize something? 
To constantly optimize for local optimality is called a "[Greedy Algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greedy_algorithm)".

This is actually the algorithm that nearly everyone follows, simply because we don't know and cannot know the "Globally Optimal" route, or even the kind of outcome possible for something that is maximally optimal.

A "Greedy" Approach has its benefits:
1. It is cheap (computationally) and easy to implement.
2. If the best possible option is not possible to discover naturally, the next best option is to find a approximation of what could be the best outcome through heuristics.

In computer science, the maximally beneficial solution (to reach an answer or goal state the fastest) is also called the ["correct" solution](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php?title=Greedy_algorithm).

It has been mathematically proven, in a deterministic system, that a Greedy Algorithm will usually never produce the "correct" solution.

If we narrow this down:

1. In the best case: Greedy performs as well as the globally optimal path.
2. In the average case: Greedy can perform within 90% of the globally optimal path (or worse).
3. In the worst case: Greedy can perform hundreds or thousands of times worse than the optimal path.

Given probability, you have a roughly equal chance of getting either the best case or worst case. On average it usually does pretty well.

Pulling this back to real life, which is again undeterministic, we can naively assume that being locally optimal gives good results most of the time, which is usually true.
But what if you are living the worst case?

There are 9 billion people in the world. All with roughly the same types of lives and careers. Is your "locally optimal" strategy actually the worst case for you?

Are you doing several hundred thousand times worse than your true potential? Your life is not deterministic. The way you approach life is even less likely to be "correct".

There is no right way to live your life. Because it is yours to live, because nobody can define what is "correct" for anyone. No one can tell you that you cannot achieve something just because you didnt hit some goal. Because you didn't grind everyday on something everyone else did.

If there is no way to find the "correct" answer, why are we so certain that the paths we optimize towards is the "right" answer?

## TLDR
I'm not telling you to suddenly prioritize happiness or joy or whimsy or curiosity over every other possible measurable metric of potential success right now. But these "metrics" are arbitrary and made out of your own biases, as well as society's.

Are you optimizing for wealth and health to be happy later on in life? Will these things make you happy even if you had them now?

Life is a unpredictable journey, take the time to smell the roses along the way. 

Even if you get pricked by thorns, maybe that’s the globally optimal path.
